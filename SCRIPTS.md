# Batch 1 — 7 Ready-to-Produce Scripts

**Niche:** Psychology of human behavior (cartoon character style)
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 30–60s (plus 2 longer ones for monetization testing)
**How to use each script:**
1. Copy the **VOICEOVER** text → paste into ElevenLabs / Clipchamp / CapCut TTS.
2. Use the **ON-SCREEN TEXT** as your caption beats (CapCut auto-captions will
   mostly match, but pin the hook manually so it shows in second 1).
3. Use the **VISUAL NOTES** to prompt your AI image tool / pick CapCut stock.
4. Post with the **CAPTION + HASHTAGS** provided.

> Reminder: keep it educational ("psychology says…", "studies suggest…"), never
> therapeutic advice. Label as AI-generated where the platform asks.

## Script 2 — "Why you remember embarrassing moments" (~40s)

**HOOK:** Your brain replays cringe on purpose.

**VOICEOVER:**
"Ever lie in bed and suddenly relive something embarrassing from ten years ago?
That's not you being broken — that's your brain doing its job. Psychologists
call it the Zeigarnik effect: your mind holds on to unresolved, emotionally
charged memories more tightly than resolved ones. Embarrassment felt socially
dangerous, so your brain flagged it as important and kept the file open. The
fix is almost funny: give the memory an ending. Silently tell yourself, 'I
survived that, and no one else even remembers.' You just closed the loop.
Comment 'closed' if you're letting one go tonight."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT BEATS:**
- Random cringe at 2am? 😬
- The Zeigarnik effect
- Unfinished = unforgettable
- Give it an ENDING
- "I survived. No one remembers."

**VISUAL NOTES:** Cartoon character in bed, thought bubble replaying a cringe
moment; an "open file folder" that then closes. Warm night-time palette.

**CAPTION:** Your brain keeps the file open until you close it 🧠 Comment
"closed" 👇
**HASHTAGS:** #psychologyfacts #overthinking #mentalhealth #selfhelp #mindset

## Script 4 — "The 2-minute rule" (~40s)

**HOOK:** The trick that ends procrastination in 2 minutes.

**VOICEOVER:**
"If you procrastinate, this one's for you. Your brain doesn't actually resist
the task — it resists the *size* of the task. 'Write the report' feels like a
mountain, so you scroll instead. The fix is the two-minute rule: shrink the
task until starting takes less than two minutes. Don't 'go to the gym' — just
put your shoes on. Don't 'write the essay' — just open the doc and type one
ugly sentence. Motivation doesn't create action. Action creates motivation.
Starting is the whole game, and starting takes two minutes. What's the one
thing you've been putting off?"

**ON-SCREEN TEXT BEATS:**
- Procrastinating? 📱
- Your brain fears the SIZE
- The 2-minute rule ⏱️
- Just put your shoes on
- Action → motivation (not the reverse)

**VISUAL NOTES:** Cartoon character staring at a giant mountain labeled with a
task; mountain shrinks to a tiny step. Shoes going on. Momentum lines.

**CAPTION:** Action creates motivation, not the other way around ⏱️ What are you
putting off? 👇
**HASHTAGS:** #productivity #procrastination #selfimprovement #habits #discipline

## Script 5 — "How to spot a fake smile" (~35s)

**HOOK:** Real smiles use a muscle you can't fake.

**VOICEOVER:**
"You can tell a genuine smile from a polite one, and it comes down to one
muscle. A real smile — psychologists call it a Duchenne smile — pulls up the
corners of the mouth AND crinkles the muscles around the eyes. A fake smile only
moves the mouth; the eyes stay still. The eye muscle is really hard to activate
on command, which is why forced smiles look a little empty. Next time you're
talking to someone, glance at their eyes. If the corners crinkle, they mean it.
If not… they're being polite. Did you know this one?"

**ON-SCREEN TEXT BEATS:**
- Real vs. fake smile 😊
- The Duchenne smile
- Real = mouth + EYES
- Fake = mouth only
- Watch the eye corners

**VISUAL NOTES:** Two cartoon faces side by side — one with crinkled eyes
(green check), one with still eyes (red x). Zoom on the eye corners.

**CAPTION:** The eyes never lie 👀 Did you know this? 👇
**HASHTAGS:** #psychologyfacts #bodylanguage #psychology #humanbehavior #dating

## Script 6 — "Why we love people who are hard to get" (~45s)

**HOOK:** Your brain wants what it can't fully have.

**VOICEOVER:**
"Ever notice you want someone more the second they pull away? There's a reason,
and it's not romantic — it's chemical. Psychologists link it to intermittent
reinforcement: unpredictable rewards spike dopamine harder than reliable ones.
It's the same mechanism that makes slot machines addictive. When someone's
attention is inconsistent, your brain treats them like a jackpot it has to keep
pulling for. Here's the reframe that'll save you years: confusing someone for a
slot machine is not love — it's a nervous system on a hook. Real connection
feels calm, not anxious. Consistency is the green flag. Save this for when you
need the reminder."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT BEATS:**
- Want them more when they pull away? 🎰
- Intermittent reinforcement
- Unpredictable = dopamine spike
- It's a slot machine, not love
- Calm > anxious. Consistency = green flag.

**VISUAL NOTES:** Cartoon character at a slot machine with a heart symbol;
dopamine sparks on a win. Cut to a calm, steady scene labeled "real connection."

**CAPTION:** Anxious isn't love — it's a nervous system on a hook 🎰 Save this
one 💚
**HASHTAGS:** #relationshippsychology #datingadvice #attachment #psychology #selfworth

## Script 7 — "3 signs of quiet confidence" (LONG — ~75s, for monetization)

> This one is intentionally over 60 seconds so it qualifies for TikTok Creator
> Rewards. Post 2–3 like this per week.

**HOOK:** Loud people aren't confident. Here's what real confidence looks like.

**VOICEOVER:**
"We're taught that confidence is loud — the person dominating the room. But
psychology suggests the most confident people are often the quietest. Here are
three signs of quiet confidence you can start building today.

One: they're comfortable with silence. Insecure people rush to fill every pause,
over-explain, and seek reassurance. Quietly confident people let a silence sit,
because they don't need your approval to feel okay. Silence doesn't scare them.

Two: they can say 'I don't know.' It sounds backwards, but admitting you don't
have the answer takes security. People who fake certainty are usually protecting
a fragile ego. The confident person is fine being a beginner in public.

Three: they celebrate other people. Insecurity makes someone else's win feel
like your loss. Real confidence has no scarcity — when you're secure, you can
hype up the person next to you without it costing you anything.

Notice these aren't personality traits — they're skills. You can practice
sitting in silence, saying 'I don't know,' and celebrating one person today.
Confidence isn't who you are; it's what you repeat. Which of the three are you
working on? Comment one, two, or three."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT BEATS:**
- Loud ≠ confident
- 1) Comfortable with silence 🤫
- 2) Can say "I don't know"
- 3) Celebrates other people 🙌
- Confidence = skills, not traits
- Which one? 1, 2, or 3?

**VISUAL NOTES:** Cartoon character calm amid a loud, chaotic crowd. Three
numbered scene cards. End on the character confidently comfortable in a quiet
frame. Consistent color palette across all three "sign" cards for series feel.

**CAPTION:** Confidence isn't who you are — it's what you repeat 🙌 Which one are
you working on? 1, 2, or 3? 👇
**HASHTAGS:** #confidence #selfimprovement #psychology #mindset #personalgrowth
